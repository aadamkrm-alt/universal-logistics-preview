IMEI scanning and inspection in Dubai

Capture of IMEI and serial numbers with physical inspection, producing a verifiable record of exactly what is inside a consignment.

Overview

An IMEI is the identifier that makes one handset distinguishable from an identical handset next to it. Once captured across a consignment, it turns a carton marked "500 units" into a list of five hundred specific devices that either are, or are not, the ones the paperwork says were sold.

For a trader in Dubai buying stock they have never physically seen, that list is the difference between trusting a supplier and verifying them. It is also what makes a dispute resolvable: without identifiers, a disagreement about what arrived is one party’s word against another’s.

What is included

What imei & inspection covers

  • IMEI capture

    The unique identifier of each handset is scanned and recorded, producing a line-by-line list of the devices physically present.

  • Serial number capture

    For non-phone electronics, manufacturer serial numbers are captured on the same basis so any serialised product can be reconciled.

  • Carton and unit count

    Cartons are counted, opened where instructed, and unit quantities confirmed against the packing list.

  • Specification check

    Model, variant, colour, storage capacity and regional specification are checked against what the documentation states.

  • Condition and seal check

    Outer cartons, inner packaging and factory seals are examined and the condition recorded, with photographs where requested.

  • Reconciliation file

    You receive the captured data in a format you can compare against your own stock file and your supplier’s declaration.

How it works

Step by step

Each stage produces something you can check, and each decision point stays with you.
  1. 01

    Consignment booked in

    The job is opened against your reference and the documentation you have supplied is loaded as the expected position.

  2. 02

    Cartons opened and counted

    Cartons are opened to the level you instruct — full, sampled, or seal-check only — and unit quantities are confirmed.

  3. 03

    Identifiers scanned

    IMEI or serial numbers are read and captured device by device.

  4. 04

    Physical inspection

    Specification, packaging condition and seal integrity are checked and recorded.

  5. 05

    Variance reported

    Anything that does not match the paperwork — short count, wrong variant, damaged packaging — is reported to you before the stock moves on.

  6. 06

    File issued

    The captured data is issued to you and retained against the consignment record.

Dubai context

Verification as a condition of the Dubai trade

Stock moving through Dubai frequently changes hands while it is in the warehouse, sometimes more than once, and often between parties in different countries who have never met. Each of those transfers is a point at which somebody is being asked to accept a description of goods they cannot see.

Scanning at the point the stock enters the free zone puts an independent record between the parties. The seller can show what was delivered. The buyer can confirm what they are acquiring. If the counts do not agree, that is established before the goods travel again, not after.

Universal carries out that work at its Dubai Airport Free Zone facility, so verification happens at the same place the stock is received and held rather than adding a separate movement to a third-party site.

Scope and limits

  • Scanning records the identifiers physically present in the consignment. It is not a check against any manufacturer, carrier, insurance or law-enforcement database, and it is not a validity or blacklist check.
  • Inspection is a check of packaging, specification and count against documentation. It is not functional testing and it is not a grading service.
  • The depth of inspection is whatever you instruct. A sampled check and a full unit-level check produce very different levels of assurance, and the difference is agreed before work starts.

Common questions

IMEI & inspection — questions we are asked

What exactly do I receive at the end of a scan?

A data file listing the identifiers captured from the consignment, alongside the count and any variance against the documentation you supplied. It is intended to be reconciled directly against your own stock records.

Does scanning tell me whether a device is blacklisted or stolen?

No. Universal records the identifiers physically present. It does not query manufacturer, carrier, insurance or law-enforcement databases, and no statement about a device’s status is made or implied.

Can you scan only part of a consignment?

Yes. Sampled checks are common where volume is high and the supplier relationship is established. What matters is that the sampling basis is agreed in advance so everyone understands what the resulting file does and does not prove.

Can you photograph the cargo as well?

Yes. Photographs of outer cartons, seals, packaging condition and unit presentation can be taken as part of the inspection and issued with the file.
